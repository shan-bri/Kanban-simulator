    Tutorial activities

    Your team runs a two-station cell making the M-187 rim. Station 1 (Machining) fills standard containers; Station 2 (Assembly) uses one rim per customer order. Assembly demand averages 48 rims per 8-hour day. A material handler moves containers between the stations, and the kanban card on each container looks like this:

    Use Save run to log after every experiment so your results are kept in the Run log tab. Unless told otherwise, simulate 10 days with seed 1.

    0 · Read the model

    10 min
    1. Load the preset Baseline: two-card, steady demand. Under Run, set Run length to 2 hours. Press Play at 5 or 15 min/s and watch the whole run.
    2. Follow one blue C-kanban all the way round: assembly opens a container → card goes to the C-mailbox → handler collects it → swaps it onto a full container at Machining → returns.
    3. Follow one amber P-kanban: removed at the outbound buffer → P-mailbox → machine queue → setup and run → back on a full container.
    Check Match what you saw to steps 1–6 of the two-card procedure in the lecture. Which step authorises movement and which authorises production?

    2. Calculate C (min and days) and Kc. Round up.

    Q2.1 Did your rounded-up kanbans give 100% fill rate? What happened when you removed one, and in which loop did the failure show up first?
    Q2.2 Compare the time study with the measured tables under the chart. Why is the measured waiting time d (C loop) or f (P loop) longer than the time study? Hint: multiply Kc × Q ÷ D and compare with the measured total C.

    3 · Variability and breakdowns

    25 min

    Real demand is not evenly spaced. Load the preset Activity 3: random demand. From now on use Run seeds 1–3 & save: the log records the worst fill rate across three different weeks of customer orders.

    1. Run Kc = 2, Kp = 2. Record the worst fill rate.
    2. Add kanbans one at a time. Find the smallest Kc + Kp that gives a worst fill rate of at least 98%. Does an extra C-kanban or an extra P-kanban help more?
    3. Load the preset Activity 3: random demand + breakdowns (machine fails on average every 4 h of running, 45 min to repair). Repeat step 2.
    4. Work out the safety factor your answer implies: X = (Kc + Kp) ÷ (D(P+C)/Q) − 1, using P and C from Activity 2.
    Q3.1 The lecture suggests starting with X ≈ 10%. How does your X compare, with and without breakdowns? What does this tell you about where safety stock really comes from?
    Q3.2 Which special card from the lecture (express, temporary or odd-number) would you use when the machine breaks down, and what would it trigger?

    4 · Improvement challenge

    25 min

    Load the preset Activity 4: improvement challenge (random demand + breakdowns). Management wants less inventory without letting customers down.

    • Goal: lowest average WIP with a worst fill rate ≥ 98% over seeds 1–3.
    • You may change: Q, Kc, Kp, and the handler's collection round (60 or 30 min).
    • One kaizen project allowed: a SMED workshop that cuts setup from 15 to 5 min.
    • Constraint: Station 1 utilisation must stay below 85%.
    • Baseline to beat: Q = 5, Kc = 4, Kp = 3.

    Save your best design in the log with seeds 1–3.

    Q4.1 Try Q = 2 with the original 15-min setup. What happens to Station 1, and why? Connect this to the lot-sizing tutorial.
    Q4.2 Toyota deliberately removes kanbans to expose problems. Which problem did removing kanbans expose in your model, and what improvement fixed it?

    Wrap-up

    10 min
    1. In one sentence: why does pull keep WIP bounded when push does not?
    2. Name two improvements that would let you safely remove a kanban from this line.
    3. What does this model leave out that a real line would have? Think about rule 4 (build-in quality), several part numbers, and the handler's capacity.
